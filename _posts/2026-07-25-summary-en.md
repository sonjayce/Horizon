## [China Releases New Offshore Trust Personal Income Tax Rules](https://liaoning.chinatax.gov.cn/art/2026/7/24/art_5869_7823.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On July 24, 2026, China&\#x27;s Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration jointly issued Announcement No. 21 of 2026, the first systematic regulation clarifying personal income tax \(PIT\) obligations for offshore trusts. The rules mandate that resident individuals pay a uniform 20% PIT on the appreciation of property transferred into offshore trusts, all gains generated during the trust&\#x27;s operation regardless of distribution, and liquidation proceeds, with a 90-day grace period for back payment of taxes owed on trusts established between 2023 and 2025 without late fees. This regulation closes long-standing tax avoidance loopholes that high-net-worth individuals used to leverage offshore trusts for wealth isolation and inheritance planning, marking a critical upgrade to China&\#x27;s cross-border tax compliance framework. It will force a major overhaul of asset structuring strategies for residents who previously relied on offshore trusts to defer or evade personal income tax obligations on cross-border assets. All taxable amounts are calculated as the appreciation value \(current market value minus original cost and reasonable expenses\), with a fixed 20% tax rate applied uniformly across all stages of the trust lifecycle including property transfer-in, operational gains, and liquidation. Management fees and other operational costs of the offshore trust are not deductible from taxable gains, and the rules also specify tax obligations for special scenarios such as a resident individual becoming a non-resident or the death of a trust beneficiary.

telegram · zaihuapd · Jul 25, 00:31

**Background**: Offshore trusts are legal structures where a settlor transfers assets to a trustee \(usually a third-party entity based in a low-tax or tax-free jurisdiction\) to manage and distribute for the benefit of designated beneficiaries, widely used by high-net-worth individuals for wealth preservation, intergenerational inheritance, and tax optimization. Before this regulation, China lacked clear, systematic rules for taxing personal income from offshore trusts, leading to widespread use of these structures to defer or avoid personal income tax on capital gains, investment returns, and other income. The new &quot;penetration taxation&quot; model treats the offshore trust as a transparent pass-through entity, directly attributing all income and gains to the resident individual settlor or beneficiary for tax purposes, aligning with global trends in cross-border tax transparency and anti-avoidance.
