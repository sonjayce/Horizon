## [Microsoft Integrates MAI-Cyber-1-Flash AI Model into MDASH Platform](https://microsoft.ai/news/introducing-mai-cyber-1-flash-inside-mdash/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Microsoft has announced the integration of its newly developed MAI-Cyber-1-Flash AI cybersecurity model into its MDASH multi-agent security scanning platform. The model is purpose-built to leverage Microsoft&\#x27;s vast corpus of real-world security signals collected across its wide range of consumer and enterprise products and services. This launch represents Microsoft&\#x27;s first dedicated cybersecurity AI model, and its cost-efficient design enables it to handle roughly 90% of routine security tasks, reserving more expensive large language models for the 10% of exceptionally complex, high-stakes threats. Microsoft&\#x27;s unique access to trillions of daily cross-product security signals \(covering identity, endpoint, cloud and network domains\) gives the model a training data advantage that most competing cybersecurity AI tools cannot replicate. MAI-Cyber-1-Flash claims a 96% score on the CyberGym cybersecurity benchmark and promises 50% cost savings compared to using larger, more expensive models for all security tasks. The MDASH platform that houses the model is currently in limited private preview, available only to Microsoft&\#x27;s internal security engineering teams and a small group of vetted external customers.

hackernews · migmartri · Jul 27, 16:52 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49072361)

**Background**: MDASH \(Multi-model Agentic Scanning Harness\) is Microsoft&\#x27;s agentic cybersecurity system that uses large language models to analyze source code, identify potential vulnerabilities, and correlate findings with real-world runtime deployment context to prioritize exploitable flaws. AI-powered cybersecurity tools automate the previously time-consuming process of scanning code and systems for vulnerabilities, drastically reducing the time between threat discovery and remediation compared to manual human-led security reviews. Microsoft&\#x27;s vast footprint across consumer and enterprise software, cloud services, and security products gives it access to a uniquely large dataset of real-world attack signals, exploit patterns, and remediation outcomes that can be used to train more accurate, effective security AI models.
